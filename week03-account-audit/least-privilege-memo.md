This document shows the logic and reason for assigning administrative access and privileges, when escalation is required and effectively outlines the evidence and documentation required to support the reasoning behind gaining administrative access . 













## When local administrator access is justified. 



Local administrator access should only be granted to personnel that require to perform system level tasks. Examples are, IT technician's, device support teams, desktop administrators, and personnel that are responsible for handling hardware. Such as managing services on devices, configuring hardware, installing software or troubleshooting system issues. Local administrator access decisions should be based on necessary documented results and business requirements rather than coincidence. Newer employees, kiosk workers, janitors, and base level workers typically perform limited business functions with standard user functions and complete their duties effectively with the content on the systems provided.





## When to escalate instead of granting admin access. 



Escalation should occur when requests for administrator access and privileges are not clearly defined or when alternative solutions may be available with the content on the user level accounts. In a majority of cases for elevation of privileges is due to software adjustments, system packaging, deployment and policy modification. At ACME, escalation should be directed towards System Administration and Administrators and or towards System support technicians. The user must also provide their role, business justification, required task, expected duration of access and any observable impact on operation. The expected outcome of the request is either approval, denial or alternative solutions that maintain standard user functions, maintaining the security of the device and applications while meeting business requirements.





## What evidence would support an exception request.

&#x20;

Temporary administrative privileges may be applied when performing one-time approved action on a system such as system configuration or data migration. Temporary administrative access should include business justification, a description of the specific task that access is required to complete, expected duration of access, documentation of changes done to the system and management approval. The request should also identify the audit records that will be used to verify the access was only used for its management approved purpose. Maintaining this document supports compliance, accountability and post-implementation review.



## Conclusion



Applying least-privilege principles reduces security risks while still allowing users to access hardware and systems and complete their required job functions and goals. Administrator access should be limited to personnel with a demonstrated and documented operational need. Escalations should be applied when privilege requirements are unclear and all exception requests should be supported by suitable business and audit documentation that is justifiable.
